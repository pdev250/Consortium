Tail delisting: kept for the Gate 2 cash test, not for NPV

Delisting lowers NPV but releases about ₹44 Cr of stock. Its inputs (revenue per tail SKU, share of demand recaptured) have no case source. Gate 1 EBITDA is missed if less than about 35% of tail demand is recaptured.

Plant C capacity: the valuable initiative left unfunded

As costed it overspends tranche 1 by about ₹5 Cr; released at month 12 it breaks the tranche 2 limit; released after Gate 2 it adds about ₹2 Cr of NPV, and at 35% recovery pays back in month 33. The decision point is month 12, on lost-order evidence.

Stress test: move four drivers together and read the result

Each combination is a full re-run of the monthly model for the recommended mix. The sliders select from a precomputed grid of 672 runs.

Decision thresholds: each driver moved alone, all others at base

Thresholds are solved by repeated re-runs of the model (bisection) and are values, not live formulas; the workbook shows the same figures on its Summary sheet. Payback alone tolerates a 2.5-month delay and an 18.5% cost overrun, but all rules together tolerate only 0.45 month and 11.6%.

Method, sources and key assumptions

How the numbers are built

A monthly model (months 0–60) turns pilot evidence into national effects on fill rate, stock, write-offs, freight and capacity. Benefits are recovered contribution, avoided costs (capped at 40% of the benefit counted) and freed-up cash valued at 11% on ACPL-owned stock. IRR is annualised from monthly flows; payback is the first month cumulative cash stays positive. Pilot costs are sunk and excluded. Network stock released counts towards the Gate 2 cash test; only the ACPL-owned part is valued in the return.

The dashboard shows precomputed results of that model and they equal the workbook's values: the stress grid, the 64-mix table, the scenarios and the thresholds are not recalculated in the browser, so they do not respond to a changed assumption. The workbook recalculates all of them from its Assumptions sheet.
